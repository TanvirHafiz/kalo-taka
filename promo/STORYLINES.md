# Kalo Taka promo: three storylines

The one thing: **a cheerful paper city builder where you get rich by bending every rule, and the news can't stop you.** What a viewer should remember: "the game where you grab ponds, bribe the permit office and flee to Canada."

All three use only the game's real screens (captured from the live build), are about 30 to 35 s at 1920x1080, 30 fps.

## A. The Ticker (recommended: the satire is the hook, and the game already speaks in headlines)

- **Device and form:** type carries the argument, as an object-to-object chain. The game's red BREAKING ticker is the carrier: each headline scrolls in, and the camera drops from the headline into the real shot that caused it.
- **What you see and feel:** one tycoon's rise, told only by the news. It feels like a mock news bulletin about a paper toy city.
- **First two seconds:** the red ticker slides across a lone tin shed on plot 1: "Local tycoon opens Tin-Shed Ghor, says it is 'for the people'".
- **Beats:** hook (tin shed headline) → tension (pond grabbed, speed money, your face on 400 walls, each a headline that becomes its shot) → reveal (title stamped: Kalo Taka Private Limited) → proof (the street grows from day to night in one push) → payoff ("Committee forms committee to investigate committee", then the Flee cutscene: "Businessman goes abroad for medical treatment") → claim ("From tin shed to sky tower. Paperwork optional.") → end card (title, English | বাংলা, play free, URL).
- **Look:** the game's paper street and kraft ground; Baloo 2 headlines against a newspaper serif for the ticker; the red ticker band is the one material.
- **Sound:** a jaunty brass and dhol news-sting bed around 110 bpm; a pop per headline, a stamp thunk on the title, one riser into the Flee.

## B. One Plot

- **Device and form:** a number lands, in one continuous take. The camera never leaves plot 1 while it grows: tin shed, semi-pucca, dotola, To-Let Bhaban, duplex, lake palace, Sky Tower, day turning to night, the Taka counter racing from ৳50 lakh to hundreds of crore. Then it pulls back to reveal the whole street, and the tycoon flees.
- **First two seconds:** a tin shed with a clothesline, the counter reading ৳50.00 lakh, and a hand-stamped "Plot #1".
- **Beats:** hook (shed + counter) → tension (a permit stamp, a speed-money envelope) → reveal (title) → proof (seven tiers, one per beat) → payoff (pull back: the whole city at night) → claim → end card.
- **Look:** the street at full bleed, a big paper counter in Baloo 2; the counter is the material.
- **Sound:** a steady rising build, one thunk per tier, a drop at the pull-back.
- Needs seven more captures (each tier on plot 1), which the debug panel can do.

## C. Mamun's Briefing (the bold one)

- **Device and form:** a character does the work, split screen collapsing to one. Manager Mamun, the game's clipboard-carrying manager, briefs "sir" (the viewer) like a fixer pitching a scheme, in Bangla first with English subtitles; each line points at a pane that grows to fill the frame.
- **First two seconds:** Mamun's face pops up beside a tin shed: "স্যার, প্রথম প্লট ফ্রি।" ("Sir, first plot is free.")
- **Beats:** hook (Mamun's offer) → tension ("the permit office already said yes") → reveal (title) → proof (grab, bribe, posters, nephews) → payoff ("Sir, a man wants to sell us a river.") → claim → end card in both languages.
- **Look:** paper panes on the kraft ground, Mamun's speech bubbles as the material, Baloo Da 2 for Bangla.
- **Sound:** a sly, plucky bed; a soft pop per speech bubble.
